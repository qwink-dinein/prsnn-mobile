A logo the whole world can love

The best-loved logos share four things: one simple shape that means something without words, one colour is enough, it still reads at 16 pixels, and a child could draw it from memory. Each idea below is tested on the app icon, at small sizes and on a photo, in the approved palette.

Face to face My pick

A p and a q turned toward each other: two people meeting, sharing one line, with a small champagne spark above them. It reads as the brand’s first letter, as two people, and as a single friendly symbol that works in any language.

It shows the product in one glance, and it is symmetrical, so it looks right everywhere from a store icon to a sticker on a café door.

The place

A lowercase p drawn as a map pin, with a champagne diamond where the pin marks the spot. Every booking happens somewhere real and public, and that place is precious.

Pins are understood worldwide and suit the safety message (public places). It is very strong as a small icon and on maps.

Leaning in

Two arcs leaning toward each other, like two people in conversation, with the moment between them held as a champagne diamond.

It is the most abstract and calm, closest to fashion and hospitality brands. It tells a quieter story but is very elegant.

Why Face to face

It is the only idea that says what prsnn is, two people meeting, while also being the brand’s letter. The shared line between the two bowls is the company itself, and the small champagne spark above them is the moment. It is symmetrical and simple enough to draw from memory, it works in one colour, and it holds up at 16 pixels.

The wordmark is Urbanist semibold to match the app; the champagne spark carries over the diamond you already liked from Hour. If you want the place and safety story first, The place is a strong second.
